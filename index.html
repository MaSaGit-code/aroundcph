Dinner: Reffen Street Food
A short trip to Refshaleøen — the old naval island just across the water — for Reffen, Copenhagen's best street food market. 50+ stalls: Nepalese, Vietnamese, French, Danish, Caribbean. Grab whatever looks good, pick up a craft beer, and sit outside by the water. Open until 10pm on Thursdays. Low effort, great vibe, no reservation needed. From Islands Brygge take bus 9A or it's a pleasant 20-min bike ride around the harbour.
Meal + drink ~130–180 DKK pp Tip: fire pits outside — warm even on a cooler May evening

Harbour walk north: Nyhavn → Amalienborg → Little Mermaid → Kastellet
A single flowing walk northward along the harbour. Start at Nyhavn — walk the north quay for the classic photo, the south quay if you want to sit with a drink. Continue to Amalienborg Palace — the royal residence, four identical palaces around an octagonal square. If you time it at noon you'll catch the changing of the guard. Then follow the waterfront to the Little Mermaid (small but iconic — manage expectations). Right next to it is Kastellet, a beautifully preserved 17th-century star-shaped fortress — still active military but open to walkers. One of the most photogenic and peaceful spots in the city, almost nobody mentions it.
All free Tip: Kastellet is the hidden gem here — the moat walk around the ramparts takes 20 min and is worth every step

Evening: Kødbyen — Burgerfest + drinks + minigolf
Head to the Meatpacking District for the full Friday night. Start with burgers at Burgerfest — no-nonsense, good quality, right in the thick of Kødbyen's energy. Then drinks: Fermentoren next door has 24 craft taps and knowledgeable staff, or ÅBEN for their own brewery. Cap the night with a round of minigolf at Camping, Boltens Gård — a bar + minigolf spot just a short walk away near Kongens Nytorv, open until midnight. Drinks, laughs, no rush.
Burgers ~150–200 DKK pp Beer ~70–90 DKK Minigolf ~100–130 DKK pp incl. drink

Morning: Christiania
Cross to Christianshavn and walk into Freetown Christiania — a self-governing commune since 1971, completely unique in Europe. Walk the main drag, see the community gardens, DIY architecture, open-air cafés. Respectful, calm visit — it's a living community, not a tourist attraction. No photography on Pusher Street. Free to enter and wander.

Lunch: Smørrebrød at Torvehallerne
Hallernes Smørrebrød inside Torvehallerne — the quintessential Danish lunch. Open-faced rye bread with classic toppings: roast beef with remoulade and fried onions, herring, smoked salmon, fried fish cake. Order 2–3 pieces each. Browse the other stalls after — fresh fish, cheese, spices, pastries. One of the best food markets in Scandinavia.
~130–180 DKK for 2–3 pieces Tip: Torvehallerne closes at 18:00 on Saturdays — lunch timing is ideal

Afternoon walk: Rosenborg Castle gardens → Christiansborg → Black Diamond
Start at Kongens Have — the public gardens around Rosenborg Castle, one of the most beautiful parks in the city in May. Walk around the castle exterior (red brick, Renaissance towers, moat). Then head south through the old city to Christiansborg Palace — the seat of Danish parliament, on its own island. Walk around the exterior and across the canal bridges. End at the Black Diamond (Det Kongelige Bibliotek) — a striking black glass extension cantilevered over the harbour. Free to walk inside: stunning atrium, canal views, good café.
All free (exteriors + Black Diamond interior)

Saturday evening — pick your vibe
Several good options depending on energy and mood:

① Nørrebro bar crawl — the most local option. Pompette on Mølegade for natural/orange wine and snacks, then drift along Ravnsborggade and Elmegade. Genuinely Copenhagen, zero tourists. Best overall pick.

② Vesterbro / Istedgade — grittier, more mixed crowd, spontaneous. Good if you want something with more edge and less curation.

③ Back to Kødbyen for drinks only — familiar territory after Friday. Fermentoren or ÅBEN, easy and reliable. Slightly repetitive but low effort if Friday ran late.

④ Tivoli evening — if he wants a proper "experience". Lit up beautifully at night, lively on Saturdays. More structured than a bar night but genuinely impressive. Worth it if energy is high.

⑤ Islands Brygge harbour front — grab beers, sit by the water at home base. Effortless. Perfect if Saturday has been packed and you want to wind down without going far.
Bars ~80–120 DKK pp Tivoli entry ~160 DKK pp (evening)

Getting around — Metro + bus covers everything. A 24hr City Pass is ~130 DKK; a 72hr pass ~230 DKK. Bikes are ideal — Copenhagen is flat and very cycle-friendly, and Islands Brygge is perfectly positioned. Most places are 15–25 min by bike from home base.

Weather note — Reffen (Thu) and Kastellet/harbour walk (Fri) are the most weather-sensitive. If Friday is poor, swap the harbour walk with the Saturday city sights route (Rosenborg → Christiansborg → Black Diamond are all sheltered or indoor-capable).
